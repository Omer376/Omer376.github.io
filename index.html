Cyclistic Bike-Share Analysis

Turning ride-level data into a membership growth strategy.

The Business Problem

Cyclistic's marketing team needed to understand how casual riders differ from annual members, since converting casual riders into members was identified as the fastest path to future growth — but no one had modeled the behavioral gap between the two groups.

Technical Solution / Architecture

I processed and cleaned over 5 million individual ride records in R, standardizing timestamps, station data, and ride types across twelve months of raw CSV exports. I then built a star-schema data model in Power BI, connecting ride facts to date, station, and rider-type dimension tables, and layered on DAX measures for ride duration, frequency, and time-of-day segmentation.

Measurable Project Impact

  • Casual riders rode 20–25% longer per trip than annual members, revealing a leisure-driven usage pattern distinct from commuter behavior.
  • 60% of casual rides occurred on weekends, pinpointing exactly when and where to target conversion campaigns.
  • Recommendations projected a 10–15% increase in membership revenue if acted on by the marketing team.

Bellabeat Fitness Case Study

Behavioral segmentation to sharpen a wellness marketing strategy.

The Business Problem

Global Electronics Retailer Sales Report

Finding the hidden profit drivers and the delivery breakdown no one had explained.

The Business Problem

Leadership needed to identify inefficiencies, hidden profit drivers, and operational gaps across products, stores, and customer behavior — including why high-revenue products weren't always the most profitable, and why delivery complaints kept piling up.

Technical Solution / Architecture

I built a star-schema model in Power BI with Sales as the fact table, a dedicated date table for consistent time intelligence, and exchange rates merged directly into Sales to standardize every figure in USD. Data was cleaned and validated in Excel and Power BI before modeling — nulls handled, duplicates removed, totals cross-checked against row counts.

Measurable Project Impact

  • $32.66M in total profit on $23.09M in total revenue — a 59% company-wide profit margin, with Computers driving $19.3M in revenue at a 58% margin.
  • Identified a hidden margin opportunity: Music, Movies & Audio Books carried the highest margin (61%) despite the lowest revenue ($3.1M) — a clear category to push harder.
  • Surfaced a data integrity issue: every country showed an identical store count of 67, flagging a likely broken relationship worth further investigation.
  • Flagged a critical operations gap: ~79% of deliveries were logged as missing, only improving 11 points since 2016 — directly informing the recommendation for deeper delivery-data analysis.

Celestial Airline Service Satisfaction

Tracing a 57% dissatisfaction rate back to the services actually driving it.

The Business Problem

Celestial Airline was fielding a wave of service complaints and needed to know whether flight service quality was actually driving overall satisfaction — and if so, which specific service factors were the real bottlenecks.

Technical Solution / Architecture

Since the dataset arrived as a single wide table with 14 service-rating columns, I used a flat schema rather than forcing a star model that didn't fit the data. I cleaned and validated the full 129,881-row dataset in Power Query, standardized rating fields, and built calculated fields in Power BI to break satisfaction down by service category, delay time, distance, and traveler type.

Measurable Project Impact

  • Overall satisfaction sat at just 3.24/5, dropping to 2.95 among the ~57% of customers who reported being dissatisfied.
  • Pinpointed the weak spot: online services rated only 2.91 vs. 3.42 for physical products — directly identifying where to focus improvement first.
  • Quantified the delay effect: satisfaction dropped as delays increased (avg. delay ~15 min), while longer flight distances correlated with higher satisfaction.
  • Delivered a clear target: recommended lifting every service rating to 3.6+ to meaningfully shift the satisfied-customer share, plus a loyalty program for the largely returning, older, business-travel customer base.
